❓ Why Is It Still 100% Manual? (The Challenge)

Urban planning is bound by legal and statutory requirements, not just visual satellite mapping! GeoAI output often struggles with irregular borders, overlapping polygons, or misalignment with actual survey lots. To meet planning standards, AI outputs must align with:

  • Cadastral Lot Boundaries: Precise legal geometry.
  • Express Land Conditions & Land Titles: Statutory land use matching.
  • Land Use Zoning: Defined under state acts and ordinances.

📈 3 GeoAI Evolutions in Geospatial

1. Semantic Segmentation

Classifies every pixel into land use categories.

2. Object Detection

Identifies specific entities (buildings, tree canopy, infra).

3. Spatial Predictive Modeling

Predicts urban growth and future land use patterns.

⚙️ The GeoAI Pipeline Workflow (Step-by-Step)

Step 1: Spatial Data Chipping

Image augmentation generating 512x512 patches with 50% overlap to preserve edge details.

Step 2: AI Model Training

Training U-Net architectures accelerated on Google Colab (T4 GPU) using PyTorch framework.

Step 3: Inference & Vectorization

Converting raster pixel predictions directly to vector layers (Shapefiles/GeoJSON) via GeoPandas and Rasterio.

Step 4: Geometric Noise Filtering

Automated post-processing to clean spatial noise, remove irregular fragments, and smooth boundaries for planning compliance.

🔬 Model Trial & Error Phases

Shows detected classes but boundaries are "blocky" and jagged. Too coarse for statutory planning needs.

PHASE 2: U-Net + ResNet34 + Overlap Patching

Current model limitations. Visual testing highlights the challenge of jagged vs sharp corners in cadastral alignment.

PHASE 3 (GOAL): U-Net++ + ResNet50 + TTA

Ultimate goal for maximum precision & statutory alignment (Currently in gradual development due to long training times).

⚠️ Current Model Limitations (Key Findings)

While the U-Net and ResNet models successfully detect land use classes overall, visual analysis reveals significant limitations in the current phase:

  • • Boundary Detection Issues: Object boundary lines often appear wavy, coarse, and misaligned with actual cadastral lots.
  • • Corner Accuracy (Sharp vs. Jagged Corners): The model struggles to maintain sharp corners at lot angles, resulting in jagged vertices that require complex manual geometric cleaning processes.

🎯 Conclusion & Future Recommendations

The integration of GeoAI into land use mapping demonstrates immense potential to drastically reduce manual digitization bottlenecks in urban planning workflows. However, bridging the gap between raw pixel classification and statutory cadastral compliance remains a critical challenge.

Key Takeaways & Next Steps:

  • 1. Advanced Architecture Transition: Moving towards deeper encoders like ResNet50 and U-Net++ combined with Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) is essential to resolve sharp corner inaccuracies.
  • 2. Post-Processing Automation: Integrating automated topology rules and Douglas-Peucker geometric simplification algorithms will bridge the gap between AI outputs and statutory cadastral standards.
  • 3. Scalable Enterprise Deployment: Transitioning the pipeline from experimental Google Colab notebooks to scalable cloud-native architectures (such as Google Earth Engine and PostGIS) will enable statewide adoption for local planning authorities.
